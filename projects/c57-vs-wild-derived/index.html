Liddell Field Research Station · Cornell University · Summer 2020

C57BL/6J vs wild-derived mice in field enclosures

The C57BL/6J mouse is the most studied mammal in biomedicine. In the summer of 2020, at Cornell's Liddell Field Research Station, we released replicate populations of 10 male and 10 female C57 mice into large outdoor enclosures, alongside control populations of wild-derived (WD) outbred house mice raised in the same lab, and tracked every animal's visits to eight resource zones by RFID for ten days. This page lets you explore the published data directly, figure by figure.

By Caleb C. Vogt, using Claude Opus 5.5

The paper. Vogt CC, Zipple MN, Sprockett DD, Miller CH, Hardy SX, Arthur MK, Greenstein AM, Colvin MS, Michel LM, Moeller AH, Sheehan MJ (2024). Female behavior drives the formation of distinct social structures in C57BL/6J versus wild-derived outbred mice in field enclosures. BMC Biology 22: 35. doi:10.1186/s12915-024-01809-0. The experiment

Two enclosures (A and B), each 15.2 × 38.1 m of open field, held eight weather-proof resource zones, each with food, water and a single entrance tube (Fig 1, Fig S1). Every mouse carried a PIT tag, and an RFID antenna underneath each resource zone recorded activity in the zone. Each trial released 10 males and 10 females (one wild-derived trial had 9 males) into one enclosure for ten days; days run noon to noon.

Priority access

Each day a mouse scores its share of same-sex time at every zone it visited, minus one if it held a majority at none of them; the priority access score is the running total. A male that held one zone all trial ends near +10. By day 10, males had split into territory holders and the rest; females had not.

After the paper's Methods, which give the first two equations and describe the third in words. M: a mouse; D, d: a day; z: a resource zone; time: the mouse's time in the zone that day, from its RFID-defined visits; SM: the mice of M's sex in the trial (usually 10). Occupancy is 0 for a zone the mouse did not visit, so a day without visits scores −1. Example from the paper: if male A spends 4 h a day in a zone that only male B also visits, for 1 h a day, A scores 0.8 each day and B 0.2 − 1 = −0.8.

Time alone and with others

Cagemates

Each female shared her trial with sisters from her lab cage (cagemates). Preference is the share of a female's time with other females that she spent with cagemates, minus the share expected by chance from how many cagemates and other females were present that day (Fig 3D).

Networks in motion

Each network is built from a window that slides through the trial an hour at a time: links join mice that shared zones within the window, thicker for a higher association index (simple ratio index). Each frame starts from the previous frame's layout and relaxes it (Fruchterman–Reingold), so mice drift together and apart as their associations change. Circles grow with the number of partners; faint rings have no partners in the window. Outlines mark groups found by Louvain community detection.

About the data

  • All numbers come from the tables deposited with the paper, cut at noon on day 10. In two trials (T006 and T007) the mice stayed in the field beyond ten days; those later data are not part of the paper and are not shown.
  • Charts follow the paper's own analysis scripts, including which mice were excluded on which days, and reproduce the published panels except where noted below. Daily network measures are the published values; the sliding-window networks are rebuilt from the deposited group-by-individual tables with the same association index (rebuilt daily networks reproduce the published degree and strength of every mouse on every day).
  • Durations. In the deposited movement-bout table, 185 bouts on 27 mouse-days record their duration in minutes rather than seconds, a quirk of how R chooses time units. Here they are converted back to seconds, which raises the tracked total from the paper's  h to  h (+1.1%). It nudges the priority access scores (Figs 2D, S3B–C; two females' day-10 scores cross zero) and time in the most used zone (Figs S2B, S3E; group means by at most 0.1 points). No result changes.
  • New mice met (Fig S4C). The original script dated each pair's first meeting by the first matching row of a table sorted by zone rather than time, so the published curves for days 1–9 run low (C57 females on day 2: 4.5 published, 8.8 corrected). The corrected values are shown; day 10, which the paper's statistics used, is unchanged.
  • Male–male encounters per day (Fig S2C). The published means skipped trial-days with no encounters (wild-derived days 4, 6 and 8); those zeros are included here.
  • Time with females (Fig 2F). Reproduces the published panel, in which a male counts from his first day with females and carries his total over a day without data. The deposited script differs slightly (wild-derived territory holders on day 10: 603 instead of 574 min).
  • Times are local (EDT). A dot's position within a zone is only for display; the data say which zone, not where in it.
